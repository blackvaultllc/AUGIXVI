THE HEXO ANSWER

After zero comes −0. Negative zero is not nothing — it is zero that has crossed to the other side and remembers it. It holds the same value and the opposite direction. Only after the gate does −1 exist. That one hidden step is why backwards calculation takes so long: most people skip the gate and get the whole negative side wrong by one.

In code: +0 faces forward. −0 faces backward. Same ground. Opposite charge. HEXO keeps both, so the billion on the left and the billion on the right balance exactly.

The lab bench — what the ingredients make together

This is the science-class part of the table. On their own the blocks are just elements. Put the right ones together and you get the stuff of everyday life — water, salt, chalk, sugar, the gentle acids in lemons and vinegar, and, when a gas gets super-heated or zapped until its electrons tear free, plasma, the fourth state of matter that lights the Sun and neon signs. Tap any card to load it onto the bench below and see its ingredients light up.

Classroom-safe & legal by design: everything here is standard school-textbook and kitchen science, taught in classes everywhere. This bench teaches what things are made of — formulas and facts. It does not give step-by-step making instructions, amounts for strong chemicals, or anything for weapons, explosives, or drugs — none of that is in HEXO. Strong acids and gases are shown as knowledge only: in a real lab they belong with a teacher, goggles, and supervision.

The frontier — the missing pieces

These boxes are not on any classroom wall. Nobody has ever made these elements — every attempt so far has come up empty. Their names below are the systematic placeholder names science uses until a real discovery earns a real name. Each piece stays locked until all 118 known elements are solved in the cube; then it opens and shows its basis — everything publicly known today, by fact, that says why this element should exist and what it should be: its predicted place, its predicted electrons, and the island of stability that the heaviest predictions cluster around.
